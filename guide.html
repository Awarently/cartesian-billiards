How to use Cartesian Billiards

Every spot on the table has a number. The app tells you which number on the cushion to aim at, so you can bank or kick to it on a real table.

1How the numbers work

The table is a grid. One unit is the distance between two diamonds.

  • X goes along the long side, from 0 at the left to 8 at the right.
  • Y goes along the short side, from 0 at the bottom to 4 at the top.
  • The bottom-left corner pocket is (0, 0). The top-right corner pocket is (8, 4).
  • The side pockets are at X = 4, the middle of each long rail.

Rail letters

Each cushion has a letter.

LetterCushionNumber along it
TTop long rail0 at the left corner to 8 at the right corner
BBottom long rail0 at the left corner to 8 at the right corner
LLeft short rail0 at the bottom corner to 4 at the top corner
RRight short rail0 at the bottom corner to 4 at the top corner

The aim point

The answer the app gives is an aim point: a rail letter and a number. Examples:

  • T4.64 means the top rail, 4.64 diamonds from the left corner.
  • R1.70 means the right rail, 1.70 diamonds up from the bottom corner.
  • B6.20 means the bottom rail, 6.20 diamonds from the left corner.

Send the ball straight at that point on the cushion. The app also says it in words, for example 0.14 past diamond 4.5.

Quick way to find a decimal between two diamonds

  • .0: on the diamond.
  • .25: one quarter of the way to the next diamond.
  • .5: halfway.
  • .75: three quarters of the way.

Find the nearest of these marks first, then move a little past it or short of it. The app gives the nearest half diamond for you, for example 0.12 short of diamond 1.5.

2Find the numbers on a real table

You must use the same orientation on the real table that the app uses. Do this before each shot:

  1. Choose your bottom rail. Stand at one long rail. That rail is B. The long rail across from you is T.
  2. Find zero. The corner pocket at your left on that rail is (0, 0). The short rail at your left is L. The short rail at your right is R.
  3. Count the diamonds. On a long rail, count from the left corner: 1, 2, 3, then the side pocket is 4, then 5, 6, 7, and the far corner is 8. On a short rail, count up from the bottom corner: 1, 2, 3, and the top corner is 4.
  4. Place the balls in the app. Estimate each ball position in diamonds from that corner. For example, a ball one diamond out from the bottom rail and two diamonds from the left corner is at (2, 1).

You can choose either long rail as B. Choose the one that makes the shot easiest to read. Then keep it the same for the whole shot.

Diamonds are on the rail top, not on the cushion

The aim point is on the edge of the cushion, directly in front of that diamond position. Do not aim at the diamond itself. From an angle, sighting at the diamond on the wood moves your line. Find the number on the wood, then look straight down to the cushion edge in front of it. That point is your target.

On a phone held upright, the app turns the table on its side so it fits the screen. The numbers do not change. Rail letters and diamond numbers stay printed on the rails, so use them, not the screen direction.

3Plan a bank shot

In a bank shot, the cue ball hits the object ball, and the object ball goes off a cushion into a pocket.

  1. Select Bank.
  2. Drag the white cue ball to its position. While you drag, the app shows the exact X and Y. To type an exact position, open Show numbers and options and use Ball positions.
  3. Drag the object ball (yellow) to its position.
  4. Tap the pocket you want. The selected pocket has a yellow ring.
  5. Select how many cushions: 1, 2 or 3. The small number on each button tells how many routes work with that many cushions. A grey 0 means none.
  6. Select a route, or leave it on Auto. Auto shows the easiest route. The routes that work are listed next to Auto, easiest first. Swipe the list sideways to see all of them.
  7. Read the aim point in the cyan box on the cushion and in large type under the table, for example T4.64 → Bottom-right pocket. With 2 or 3 cushions there is one cyan box for each cushion, in order: T6.01 → R2.27 → B4.90 → Top-left pocket. Aim at the first number. The others show where the ball must go next, so you can check the path.
  8. Shoot. Hit the object ball so it travels to the aim point. To do that, aim the cue ball at the ghost ball: the dashed white circle that touches the object ball.

Why the ghost ball matters

To send the object ball in a direction, the cue ball must be at the ghost ball position when the two balls touch. Aim the centre of the cue ball at the centre of the ghost ball. Do not aim at the object ball.

4Read the result

Lines and marks on the table

White lineCue-ball path
Yellow lineObject-ball path
Cyan dot and boxAim point on the cushion, with its number
Dashed white circleGhost ball: where the cue ball must be when it hits the object ball
Red dashed lineThe shot does not work. A red ring marks a ball that blocks the path.

Status

  • Clear The path is open.
  • Caution The shot works but is risky, for example a very thin cut or a possible double kiss. The reason is shown under the table.
  • Blocked Another ball is in the path.
  • No route The geometry does not work. The reason is shown, for example The ball meets the bottom rail before the pocket.

Cushion count and route list

  • 2 with a cyan number, for example 3: three different 2-cushion routes work. A grey 0: none work.
  • Auto Shows the easiest route. The easiest route in the list has a cyan outline.
  • Each route in the list shows its cushion numbers and a difficulty: Easy, Moderate, Difficult or Advanced. Tap a route to show it. It stays selected while you move balls, and turns red if it stops working.
  • If no route works with the selected count, the list tells you which count to try, for example Try 3 cushions (1 route).

The difficulty is an estimate from the distance the balls roll, the number of cushions, and how thin the cut is. It is not a percentage chance of making the shot.

Why a route is not listed

Open Show numbers and options. Under Numbers, each route that does not work is listed with its reason. Common reasons:

  • The top-left pocket is on the top rail: the last cushion cannot be one of the rails that forms the target pocket.
  • The cue ball cannot reach the contact side of the object ball: the object ball would have to go back toward the cue ball. The cut is 90° or more.
  • The ball meets the top rail before the pocket: the ball comes in too flat along a rail and hits the cushion short of the pocket. The app's pocket sizes are estimates, so on a real table some of these still go in.
  • The contact is inside a pocket opening: the ball would go into a different pocket on the way.

5Plan a kick shot

In a kick shot, the cue ball goes off a cushion first, then hits the object ball. Use it when a ball blocks the direct line.

  1. Select Kick.
  2. Place the cue ball and the object ball.
  3. Select 1, 2 or 3 cushions, and a route, or leave it on Auto.
  4. Read the aim point. This is where the cue ball must hit the first cushion. The app aims for a full hit on the object ball.
  5. To also make the ball, select Pocket the ball, then tap a pocket. The app then aims the cue ball at the ghost ball, so it hits the object ball on the correct side to send it to that pocket.

6Do the math in your head

At the table you do not have the app in your hand. These rules give the same numbers with simple arithmetic. The app shows the rule for each route in the dashed box under the result, with your numbers filled in, for example In your head (from R): 7.0 − 2×2.0 = 3.0 → B5.0.

One cushion: the weighted average

Measure two things for the ball and for the target (the pocket, or the ball you want to hit):

  • its position along the cushion you bank off, and
  • its distance from that cushion.

Aim = (ball position × target distance + target position × ball distance) ÷ (ball distance + target distance)

The aim point is always between the ball and the target, closer to whichever is nearer the cushion. If both are the same distance from the cushion, aim exactly halfway between them.

Example. Ball at (2, 1), bottom-right pocket at (8, 0), bank off the top rail. Ball: position 2, distance 3. Pocket: position 8, distance 4. Aim = (2 × 4 + 8 × 3) ÷ 7 = T4.57.

Corner-pocket shortcut. To bank into the top-left pocket off the bottom rail: B = x × 4 ÷ (y + 4). A ball at (3, 2) gives 12 ÷ 6 = B2.0.

The start number, S

Rules 1 and 2 below need one more number. Hold your cue along the line you plan to shoot. Follow that line backward, past the ball, to the rail behind it. The diamond number where it crosses is the start number, S. The app draws this as a dotted line with a white S marker.

S is about the line, not the ball. The ball can be anywhere on that line.

Rule 1: equal steps (cross the table and back)

Use it when the ball goes from one long rail to the other and back, for example top, bottom, top.

Step = Aim − S. Each cushion after the first is one more step along.

Example. S = 0.5, aim T2.5. Step = 2.0. The ball hits B4.5, then T6.5.

To reach a spot X after k cushions: Aim = S + (X − S) ÷ (k + 1). With one cushion that is the halfway point between S and X.

Rule 2: around the corner (three cushions)

Use it when the ball goes long rail, short rail, long rail, for example top, right, bottom. This is the usual way out of a safety at the far end of the table.

  1. Count from the short rail you go around. If you go around the right rail, every long-rail number n becomes 8 − n. B1 becomes 7, T6 becomes 2, B5 becomes 3. Around the left rail, the numbers stay as they are.
  2. Land = Start − 2 × Aim.
  3. To hit a spot, turn it round: Aim = (Start − Land) ÷ 2.

Rule 3: count and subtract (two cushions around a corner)

Use it when both balls are along the same long rail and the ball goes to the far long rail, then the short rail, and comes back to the target. For example: cue ball and object ball along the bottom rail, kick top then right.

  1. Count from the short rail you go around. Along your rail, that corner is 0. Count to the cue ball (b) and to the target (t).
  2. Subtract: b − t.
  3. Halve it. Aim that many diamonds from the same corner, on the far rail.

Example. Going around the right rail. The cue ball is 6 diamonds from the right corner, the object ball is 1. 6 − 1 = 5. Half is 2.5. Aim 2.5 diamonds from the right corner on the top rail, which is T5.5.

Where the cue ball sits does not change the answer: only the gap between the two balls counts. The rule is exact when both balls are the same distance off the rail. If one is much further out, the app says so; use its aim point.

The "distance minus cue number" system. Some players count the cue ball from the far corner (c) and the gap to the target (d), and aim at d − c. The app shows that number too. It matches this rule only when the gap is twice the cue number; elsewhere it can be half a diamond off, which players make up with spin and speed. On your own table, see which one your shots follow.

What these rules do not include

The rules follow the centre of a point-sized ball. With a real ball the app's numbers move by up to about 0.3 diamond, and the app tells you the difference. Speed and spin move the ball more than that. On your own table, note how far the ball lands from the rule, for example "Rule 2 lands half a diamond short". That one correction then works for every shot of that type on that table.

7Kick drill

Practise finding the first aim point without seeing the answer.

  1. Tap Drill at the top of the screen. The app sets up a safety: ball 2 blocks the direct shot from the cue ball to ball 1.
  2. Read the task. It names the cushions to use, for example the top, right and then bottom cushions.
  3. Work out your aim with the rules above.
  4. Tap that point on the cushion. An orange marker shows your aim, for example You T5.80. Tap again to move it.
  5. Tap Check. The app shows the answer, how far off you were in diamonds, the full path, and the rule worked out with the real numbers.
  6. Tap Next layout for another, or Exit drill to go back to your own table layout.

The drill keeps a count on this device: how many you have done, how often you picked the right cushion, and your average error. Within 0.15 diamond shows in green.

9What the numbers do not include

The app uses ideal geometry: the ball leaves the cushion at the same angle it comes in. A real table is different:

  • Speed. Hard shots push into the cushion and come off shorter. Soft shots are closer to the number.
  • Spin. Side spin changes the angle off the cushion. Top and bottom spin change the path after contact.
  • Throw. In a cut, friction pushes the object ball a little away from the ghost-ball line.
  • Cushions and cloth. Every table plays a little differently.

Use the aim point as your starting line. Shoot at medium speed with no side spin. If the ball goes long or short, note how much, in diamonds, and adjust. A later version will learn these corrections for your own table.

The pocket openings in the app are estimates. A shot near a pocket jaw may show No route when it can go in on a real table, or the reverse.

10Install and use offline

  1. On iPhone or iPad, open awarently.github.io/cartesian-billiards in Safari.
  2. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open the app once while you have a connection. After that it works with no connection, including this guide.

On Android, open the same address in Chrome and tap Install app.
